What systematic trading is — and why algorithms beat hands

Rules, not vibes

In a system, every decision — what to trade, how much, when to exit — is written in code before a single trade happens. No mid-trade “feeling”: the rule decides, and the rule can be audited.

A backtest is a hypothesis

A backtest runs the rules on historical data to estimate how the system would have behaved. It's a study tool — not a crystal ball. Past results owe you nothing, and anyone claiming otherwise is selling something.

Why not manual?

Humans trade with fear, greed and fatigue. An algorithm applies the same rule to trade one thousand with the same discipline as trade one. The methodology we follow (Robert Carver) sizes positions by risk and diversifies for real.

Gurus post screenshots. Systems publish everything.

A profit screenshot isn't evidence — it's marketing. Evidence is open methodology, explained rules, and a published curve with wins AND losses, unedited. That's what serious education looks like.
